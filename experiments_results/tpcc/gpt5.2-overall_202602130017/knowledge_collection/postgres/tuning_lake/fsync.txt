{'suggestion': 'Keep `fsync` enabled for production systems that require crash-safe durability, since it forces PostgreSQL to flush data to disk so it remains durable after a crash. Only disable `fsync` for throwaway/rebuildable clusters (e.g., dev, temporary, or benchmark workloads) where you can tolerate total data loss and potential unrecoverable corruption after a crash or power loss; disabling it may improve write performance (sometimes ~2× or more). If you disable `fsync` and later re-enable it, ensure any previously dirty OS buffers are flushed first (e.g., run `initdb --sync-only`, perform an OS-level sync, or reboot).'}
