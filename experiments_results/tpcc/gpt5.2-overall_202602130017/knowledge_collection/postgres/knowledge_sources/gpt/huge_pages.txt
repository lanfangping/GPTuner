Set `huge_pages` to `on` (or `try`) when your PostgreSQL instance has a large, mostly fixed shared memory footprint—typically when `shared_buffers` is several GB (e.g., ≥4–8 GB)—and configure the OS huge pages to at least cover `shared_buffers + (max_connections × work_mem × expected_concurrent_workers) + maintenance_memory` (rule of thumb: allocate ~5–10% headroom; e.g., for `shared_buffers=16GB` with 2 MB huge pages, reserve about `16GB / 2MB ≈ 8192` pages, or ~9000 pages with headroom).
