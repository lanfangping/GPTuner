Set `wal_writer_flush_after` to the amount of WAL data (in bytes, default `1MB`, `0` disables) that should trigger the WAL writer to proactively `fsync()` WAL; for low-latency durability on busy systems a common starting point is **`1–16MB`**, while for maximizing throughput you can raise it to **`16–64MB`** (or set **`0`** to rely only on checkpoints/commit fsyncs), and then adjust based on whether you see commit/fsync stalls versus unnecessary background flush I/O.
