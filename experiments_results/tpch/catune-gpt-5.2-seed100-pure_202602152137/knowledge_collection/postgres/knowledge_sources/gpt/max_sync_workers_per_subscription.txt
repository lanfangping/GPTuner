Set `max_sync_workers_per_subscription` to (at most) the number of *concurrent table syncs you actually want* during initial/subsequent synchronization—start with **2–4**, raise to **8–16** only if you have enough CPU/IO headroom, and never exceed what you can support given that each sync worker is a background worker and must fit under PostgreSQL’s global limits (notably **`max_logical_replication_workers`** and **`max_worker_processes`**, e.g., if `max_logical_replication_workers=16` you cannot effectively set this above **16**, and in practice you’d keep it a few workers below that to leave capacity for other replication work).
